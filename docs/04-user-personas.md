# User Personas

Five primary personas for RealCost MVP — ages **24–36** (late Gen Z + Millennials), mixed employment types. Each represents a distinct path through **price → hours → decision → share → comparison**.

These are research-backed archetypes for design and positioning. They are **not** claims of live user segments or measured market share.

---

## Context / Problem

RealCost must feel instantly relevant to people who:

- Think in paychecks, not portfolios
- Compare purchases with roommates, partners, or group chats already
- Care about privacy when money is involved
- Want a pause, not a lecture

Personas anchor UX decisions: onboarding length, share defaults, tone copy, and what “success” looks like in the product.

## Persona 1 — Maya Chen

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Age** | 24 |
| **City vibe** | Austin — shared house, thrifted furniture, bouldering gym on weekends |
| **Status** | Senior undergrad + part-time at a home-goods retailer |
| **Income** | ~$16–18/hr take-home, ~20 hrs/week during semesters |

### Spending triggers

- “Treat yourself” after brutal exam weeks
- TikTok viral gadgets under $80
- Splitting DoorDash with roommates without doing the math first
- Festival tickets and last-minute outfits

### Emotional job-to-be-done

*“Make abstract prices feel like shifts I actually stand on my feet for — without making me feel stupid for wanting things.”*

Maya doesn't need a budget app. She needs a **gut-check** before Apple Pay wins.

### How she shares

- iMessage group with two roommates: *“lol this lamp is 4 hours each for us”*
- Instagram Close Friends screenshot of the share card
- Rarely adds her name to the URL — prefers anonymous curiosity

### Success moment

She types $129 for headphones, sees **7h 31m**, taps **Thinking**, and actually waits until next paycheck. Roommate opens the link, enters their wage, replies *“ok that's literally my whole Friday shift”* — they both skip.

### Design implications

- **Fast first calc** — no account, minimal onboarding steps
- **Shift framing** secondary to hours (retail thinks in shifts)
- **Skip decision copy** must feel like a win, not deprivation
- **Share card** must look good in Close Friends — premium, not spreadsheet

## Persona 2 — Jordan Okonkwo

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Age** | 28 |
| **City vibe** | Brooklyn — studio with partner, subway commute, meal-preps but splurges on tech |
| **Status** | Full-time UX researcher at a mid-size SaaS company |
| **Income** | ~$72k salary (~$34/hr take-home after taxes/benefits — thinks in monthly net) |

### Spending triggers

- New Apple / mechanical keyboard drops
- Weekend trips booked on impulse during Slack downtime
- Subscription creep (Figma, Notion, streaming, gym)
- Gifts for partner that “aren't that expensive” but add up

### Spending triggers (behavioral)

Salary makes numbers feel softer until converted to hours. Jordan still **emotionally budgets in time**.

### Emotional job-to-be-done

*“Sanity-check big purchases and show my partner what ‘expensive’ means to me — without putting my salary in a group chat.”*

### How they share

- Sends `/c` link to partner before buying a $400 monitor: *“This is 11 hours for me — what's it for you?”*
- Uses **Straight up** tone — hates cheerleader energy on money
- **Shared** tab as private log of “conversations we almost had”

### Success moment

Partner opens comparison, sees side-by-side hours, realizes the gap isn't about discipline — it's income structure. They pick a cheaper monitor together. No wage was ever in the URL.

### Design implications

- **Privacy messaging** on share flow — explicit “your rate stays on this device”
- **Side-by-side compare view** is the hero for `/c` — clean, no clutter
- **Optional name** in URL — off by default, easy toggle
- **Subscription category** chip for recurring cost moments

## Persona 3 — Priya Sharma

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Age** | 26 |
| **City vibe** | Chicago — grad student apartment near campus, coffee shop thesis sessions |
| **Status** | PhD candidate (public health) + research assistant stipend |
| **Income** | ~$2,200/month stipend (~$14/hr equivalent if treating as 40hr — but actually irregular 50hr weeks) |

### Spending triggers

- Conference travel “required” for career
- Replacing broken laptop peripherals
- Saying yes to dinner with cohort (social debt)
- Anxiety purchases: productivity apps, planners, noise-canceling gear

### Emotional job-to-be-done

*“Translate stipend guilt into a concrete number so I can decide if this purchase is worth the hours I'm already giving the lab.”*

### How she shares

- Group chat with labmates comparing flight costs for a conference
- Uses **Gently** tone — shame-sensitive after years of academia scarcity mindset
- Screenshots share card for Twitter/X with caption about stipend life (no wage shown)
- Checks **Shared** tab after roommate reacts

### Success moment

$320 flight shows **22h+** of stipend time. She logs **Thinking**, checks **Shared** after roommate reacts — roommate's hours are lower (partner works full-time). Priya feels validated, not judged, and picks a cheaper flight time.

### Design implications

- **Custom hourly rate** editable anytime in Settings — stipend users fudge the number
- **Travel + Tech categories** prominent
- **Gently tone default** in onboarding for shame-prone users
- **History filters** — review Thinking pile during monthly money anxiety

## Persona 4 — Devon Reyes

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Age** | 30 |
| **City vibe** | Los Angeles — roommate situation in Koreatown, gig schedule changes weekly |
| **Status** | Part-time bartender + freelance motion designer (project-based) |
| **Income** | ~$22–35/hr blended — highly variable week to week |

### Spending triggers

- Gear upgrades (camera, lenses) that “pay for themselves”
- Going out after closing shifts (cash feels like play money)
- Car maintenance and parking tickets
- Impulse ASOS carts at 2am post-shift

### Emotional job-to-be-done

*“Use a conservative hourly number so impulse buys survive daylight — and send something funny-real to friends who don't get gig income swings.”*

### How they share

- TikTok stitch: share card + reaction face
- Group chat with service-industry friends — everyone uses different “effective hourly”
- **Hyped** tone — wants energy, not therapy

### Success moment

$180 sneakers = **8h** at Devon's conservative rate. Sends link to bartender friend. Friend's hours come back nearly double. They meme about it, both log **Skipped**, Devon adds to weekly skip summary — feels like a game, not judgment.

### Design implications

- **Settings: edit rate easily** — gig workers update after slow weeks
- **Hyped + Curious tones** must feel authentic, not corporate
- **Share card** optimized for vertical video screenshot (9:16 safe zone)
- **Weekly skip summary** in decision copy — reinforcement without streaks

## Persona 5 — Alex Kim

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Age** | 34 |
| **City vibe** | Denver — townhouse with spouse, dog, weekend hikes |
| **Status** | Full-time public high school teacher |
| **Income** | ~$52k salary (~$24/hr take-home — stable, predictable, not high) |

### Spending triggers

- Classroom supplies paid out of pocket (small but frequent)
- Kids' activities and birthday gifts
- “We deserve it” home upgrades after hard semesters
- Summer travel planning in January optimism

### Emotional job-to-be-done

*“Show my spouse what a ‘small’ purchase costs in teaching hours — and decide together without a budget spreadsheet war.”*

### How they share

- iMessage to spouse with comparison link before buying a $600 patio set
- Uses **Curious** tone — prefers questions over declarations
- History tab monthly review: how many **Thinking** items became **Bought**

### Success moment

Patio set shows **25h** for Alex. Spouse opens link — different job, different hours. They realize the purchase isn't wrong, but timing is. Log **Thinking**, revisit in June. **Shared** preserves the moment without argument.

### Design implications

- **Large touch targets** — often uses phone one-handed while cooking
- **Light mode** via OS preference — bright kitchen environments
- **Others category + custom label** for classroom supplies
- **No leaderboard, no streaks** — Alex would uninstall immediately
- **Reset onboarding** without wiping history — spouse tries app on same iPad

## Anti-Persona (MVP)

**Not designing for these users in v1:**

| Anti-persona | Why excluded |
|--------------|--------------|
| Parents managing household budgets at scale | Need aggregation, accounts, categories across family — out of scope |
| Enterprise finance / FP&A teams | Wrong product category entirely |
| Minors | Wage context inappropriate; no COPPA-ready flows |
| High-net-worth optimizing investments | RealCost is purchase-moment, not portfolio |

---

## Cross-Persona Design Requirements

| Requirement | Personas served |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Wage never in URL | All — especially Jordan, Alex |
| <60s to first result | Maya, Devon (impulse moments) |
| Four tone options | Priya (Gently), Jordan (Straight up), Devon (Hyped), Alex (Curious) |
| Share card screenshot-ready | Maya, Devon, Priya |
| `/c` side-by-side without account | Jordan, Alex |
| localStorage-only privacy story | All |

---

## Outcomes / Success Criteria

Persona work succeeds when:

- Onboarding examples resonate in user interviews (no “this isn't for me”)
- Share copy is sent peer-to-peer without explanation
- Each persona has a documented **success moment** the UI can be tested against
- Design reviews cite persona name + implication, not generic “users”

## Risks / Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Stipend vs hourly mental model | Priya may enter fictional “effective rate” — UI should not police this |
| Gig income volatility | Devon needs easy rate edits; no rolling averages in MVP |
| Couple sharing one device | Alex scenario — reset onboarding path must preserve history |
| Creator distribution | Devon's TikTok path may drive recipients who never set wage — `/c` must stand alone |
